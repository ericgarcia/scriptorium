# Talk

A talk is a piece whose reader is a room. It is drafted the way an essay is — the argument
in paragraphs, steered by a style — and the deck falls out of the draft rather than being
built beside it. **One file, two products:** the paragraphs are what the speaker says; the
lines that are not paragraphs are what the room sees.

## The dialect (all of it)

```
*Draft — v1, YYYY-MM-DD. Header above the first --- is scaffold and never renders.*
---
## I. The Setup                        <- a movement: renders as a section-title slide
<!-- slide: The promise, stated fairly -->   <- a slide, with its title
> Enough attributes and the right person is a query away.    <- on the slide (blockquote)
- one bullet                           <- on the slide (list)
![Figure 0](assets/fig0.png)           <- on the slide (image)
The spoken script goes here, in ordinary paragraphs. It becomes the slide's
presenter notes and is never shown to the room.
<!-- slide -->                         <- an untitled slide (a figure alone, a beat)
```

That is the whole grammar. `python3 framework/tools/md_to_marp.py pieces/<slug>` reads it with
`talk.yaml` (title, subtitle, speaker, duration, theme, size) and writes `deck.md`, a Marp
deck with the script as notes. Render with
`npx -y @marp-team/marp-cli deck.md -o deck.html` (or `-o deck.pdf`), from inside the piece
directory so the figure paths resolve. `--check` refuses on a figure the draft names that is
not on disk, and on a slide with no script over it.

## Always do this first

1. Identify the piece and its **target style** from `pieces/<slug>/README.md`. A talk wants a
   **spoken-register** style — one whose constitution says how a sentence lands on the ear
   once, at speaking pace — not the page voice unchanged. If the desk has none, say so and
   stop; a constitution is a `tune-style` decision, not a drafting one.
2. Load the style as `draft` does: `style.md`, `config.yaml` (honor `pace_wpm`,
   `slide_text_max_words`, `bullets_max`, the `avoid` list literally), `exemplars/`.
3. Read `outline.md` — a talk outline carries **timings per movement**; the draft owes each
   movement its minutes, at the style's words-per-minute, and not more. Read `notes.md` for
   the citations; a talk cites less than an essay and must be exact where it does.
4. Read `talk.yaml`. Duration, venue and audience live there, not in the prose.

## Drafting

- **Say it, then show it.** A figure is introduced in the script before the slide that carries
  it, and read aloud after: what the axes are, where to look, what it means. The room cannot
  study a chart while listening to a sentence.
- **One claim per slide; the slide holds what the ear cannot** — a figure, a number, a
  citation, a single line the speaker wants remembered. Slide text is never the script and the
  script never reads the slide.
- **The movement boundary is spoken once**, in one sentence, and the slide's section title
  does the rest. No "in this section I will."
- **Land each movement on one line** the audience could repeat in the hallway. The outline
  usually names it; the draft owes it.
- **Figures are code.** Every figure the talk needs is produced by a script in
  `pieces/<slug>/assets/` (deterministic seed, one palette, one aspect ratio) and the output is
  checked in beside it. A figure that cannot be regenerated cannot be corrected; a figure that
  is not checked in is not in the piece. Name the figure in the outline first, generate it,
  then write the slide that shows it — the draft never references a figure that does not
  exist.
- **Budget by the clock.** `md_to_marp.py` prints spoken words and minutes at the style's pace;
  a movement over its outline minutes is a cut, not a faster delivery.
- Write into `pieces/<slug>/draft.md`; one current draft; git remembers the rest.

## After a drafting session

1. `python3 framework/tools/md_to_marp.py pieces/<slug> --check` — zero faults, and the minute
   count against `talk.yaml`'s `duration_min`.
2. Render the deck once and look at it: a figure that is unreadable at slide size is a figure
   to regenerate, not to caption around.
3. `check_pronouns.py` on the draft as for any piece (an invented person is *they/them*; the
   house's deity conventions stand wherever God is named).
4. Log the session (dated, append-only), update the piece README's `Stage:` and `Next move:`,
   refresh the dashboard fragment, `dashboard.py sync`.

## Boundaries

- Never edit `styles/<style>/style.md` or `config.yaml`; wording feedback goes to
  `corrections.md`.
- Never invent a citation, a number, or a study result. The talk's sources are in `notes.md`
  or they are `[bracketed]` for the author. A number spoken to a room is harder to retract
  than one printed.
- The deck is a rendering of the draft. Never edit `deck.md` by hand — it is regenerated; an
  edit there is lost at the next run and is not in the piece.
