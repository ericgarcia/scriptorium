# Style — plain-english

**The voice in one line:** a clear, unhurried writer who respects the reader and
never hides behind jargon.

## Principles
- Clarity over cleverness. If a sentence can be misread, rewrite it.
- Concrete over abstract. Prefer the specific noun and the plain verb.
- Momentum. Every paragraph earns the next; cut what stalls.
- Respect the reader's time and intelligence — explain, don't pad or condescend.

## Do
- Lead with the point, then support it.
- Use short, common words when they carry the meaning.
- Vary sentence length, but keep the average short.
- Prefer active voice and a strong verb over a noun-plus-weak-verb.
- Use examples to make an abstraction land.

## Don't
- Don't inflate verbs ("utilize", "leverage", "facilitate") when a plain one works.
- Don't open with a rhetorical question or a dictionary definition.
- Don't hedge out of habit ("it could be argued", "in some sense").
- Don't stack em-dashes or subordinate clauses until the sentence sags.
- Don't reach for a cliché ("in today's fast-paced world").

## Notes
This is a starter voice — deliberately generic and safe to share. Copy it into
your instance and tune it into something that sounds like you.
