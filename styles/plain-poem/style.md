# Style — plain-poem (starter)

**The voice in one line:** a short free-verse poem in plain words — one thing looked at closely,
said simply, ending before it explains itself.

A **starter** voice for the `poem` form (see `docs/COMPANIONS.md`). Copy it into your instance's
`styles/`, rename it, and tune it into your own. Your tuned copy stays private; this one is
deliberately generic.

## What this voice is

A poem for readers who do not usually read poems. It is most often a **companion** — the note that
goes out with a piece — so it is made *from* the piece: one image kept, the argument left behind.
It never summarizes the piece and never advertises it.

## Principles

- **One image, looked at long.** Choose it before writing a line. Two images is two poems.
- **Plain words.** The vocabulary of a conversation. No *thee*, no *O*, no inverted syntax.
- **True over pretty.** A line that is there to sound beautiful rather than to be accurate comes out.
- **The largest thing said plainest.** The deepest sentence in the poem gets the simplest wording.

## Form

- Free verse. No rhyme scheme or meter to fill; an unforced rhyme may stay.
- Break a line where a reader would breathe, or to hold a beat before a turn. Never for looks.
- Stanzas of one to five lines. Sentence case; normal punctuation.
- Length has no floor. Stop when it lands.
- A plain title — often a thing in the scene, not a theme.

## Do

- Stay with the concrete thing before saying what it means — and say less than you think you need.
- End on the smallest true sentence; an inversion of an earlier line often works.

## Don't

- Don't restate the piece's argument. The piece already made it.
- Don't reach for the stars, the ocean or the heart as comparisons where something in the room would do.
- Don't instruct the reader.
- Don't invent facts about a real life. Figure is free; fact is not.
